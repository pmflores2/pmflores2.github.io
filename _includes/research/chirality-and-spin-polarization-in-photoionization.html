Project description:

Chiral molecules are molecules that can exist in two mirror-image forms, much like our left and right hands. These two forms, called enantiomers, have the same chemical composition but differ in their handedness. Chirality is especially important in biology and chemistry, where the two enantiomers of a molecule can interact differently with other chiral systems and can therefore have very different biological effects.

Being able to distinguish between enantiomers is therefore important, motivating the search for new ways to detect and characterize molecular handedness. One approach is to ionize a molecule with light and examine the emitted electron. The direction in which the electron is emitted can already carry information about the molecule's chirality.

In this project, we explore an additional property of the emitted electron: its spin. We ask whether the spin polarization of photoelectrons can provide new observables that are sensitive to molecular handedness, and what determines these spin-dependent signatures. By studying randomly oriented molecules in the gas phase, we identify which spin-polarization effects survive molecular orientation averaging and can therefore serve as signatures of chirality in an isotropic sample.

What we do (the technical version)

The workhorse observable is photoelectron circular dichroism (PECD): with circularly polarized light, randomly oriented chiral molecules emit electrons with a forward-backward asymmetry along the light's propagation direction that flips sign between enantiomers. We extend this to spin-resolved one-photon ionization, within the electric-dipole approximation (no magnetic interactions of the light field needed).

  • CISS as a conditioned measurement. Symmetry only requires chiral molecules to support correlations between spin and electron-current vectors. We show that CISS is fundamentally a conditioned measurement of these spin-current correlations: look at the electrons with a given spin, and the chiral signal appears.
  • Geometric origin. The spin- and enantio-sensitive observables come from two intrinsic mechanisms, quantified by two pseudovectors built from the geometry of the photoionization dipoles in real space and in spin space, plus an extrinsic directional bias set by the light polarization. This lets us revisit Cherepkov's 1983 prediction of ten independent parameters and reduce them to moments of three pseudovectors.
  • New chiral observables. In a synthetic chiral argon toy model, the spin-polarized signals are comparable to PECD and can even exceed it. They include enantio-sensitive spin vortices in the plane of the light polarization that rotate in opposite directions for opposite enantiomers.
  • Spin and orientation. Related work (the "molecular compass") connects the photoelectron spin to the orientation of the remaining cation, in an enantio-sensitive way.
